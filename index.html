Our mission

Project Pulmonary is a 501(c)(3) public health nonprofit organization focused on lung cancer prevention in firefighters, specifically through pulmonary rehabilitation for those chronically exposed to wildfire carcinogens.

Why it matters

Wildfire Smoke and Firefighter Cancer Risk

Group 1

The WHO's cancer agency classified firefighting as carcinogenic to humans in 2022, alongside tobacco and asbestos.

75%

of firefighters honored at the IAFF's 2022 Fallen Fire Fighter Memorial died of occupational cancer, not injury.

~20%

of PAH exposure is absorbed through the skin, so turnout gear alone isn't enough.

What’s in the smoke

Volatile organic compounds (VOCs)

Fire smoke carries dozens of VOCs that crews breathe in and absorb through their skin. These six are among the most studied.

Benzene

C6H6

IARC Group 1 carcinogen, linked to leukemia.

Formaldehyde

CH2O

IARC Group 1. Irritates the eyes, nose and airways.

1,3-Butadiene

C4H6

IARC Group 1. Released when wood and plastics burn.

Acrolein

C3H4O

One of the harshest respiratory irritants in smoke.

Toluene

C7H8

A solvent vapor that can cause headaches and dizziness.

Styrene

C8H8

Given off by burning plastics. IARC Group 2A.

Sources: IARC Monographs; U.S. EPA, Wildfire Smoke: A Guide for Public Health Officials.
