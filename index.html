Protect Those

Project Pulmonary is a 501(c)(3) public health nonprofit organization that focuses on lung cancer prevention in firefighters by providing pulmonary rehabilitation following carcinogenic inhalation of wildfire VOCs. Through hydration item donations, detoxification sauna installations, K-8 lung cancer screening letters, and student-led chapters, we turn awareness of long-term smoke exposure into direct, visible support.

The Science

Smoke doesn't stop when the flames go out.

Every structure and wildland fire releases a toxic cocktail of fine particulates, PAHs, benzene, and formaldehyde. Firefighters breathe it in, and it absorbs straight through turnout gear and skin.

01

Inhalation

Microscopic PM2.5 particles, roughly 30x smaller than a human hair, bypass the body's natural filters and travel straight into the deepest airways.

02

Cellular damage

Carcinogens like benzene and PAHs trigger oxidative stress that breaks down cell walls and sets off chronic inflammation in lung tissue.

03

Repeated exposure

Firefighters face this cycle fire after fire, year after year, with damage compounding faster than the body can repair it.

04

Long-term disease

Over a career, this exposure is linked to elevated rates of lung cancer, cardiovascular disease, and other chronic respiratory illness.

0 of firefighter line-of-duty deaths are linked to occupational cancer.
Two Frontlines

Different fires. The same invisible risk.

Structural and wildland firefighters take on the same long-term exposure risk through very different calls.

Structural firefighters

First through the door of every burning building.

Career and volunteer firefighters responding to structure fires face a modern hazard their predecessors didn't: homes and offices built from synthetic materials that burn hotter and dirtier, releasing higher concentrations of carcinogens with every call.

Wildland firefighters

Weeks on the fire line, breathing smoke the whole way.

Wildland crews can spend weeks on an active fire, often without respiratory protection built for wildfire conditions. Research links this repeated exposure to markedly elevated lung cancer and cardiovascular disease risk.
