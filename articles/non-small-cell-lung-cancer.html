Lung Cancer

Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer, or NSCLC is a type of lung cancer that can cause constant coughing, hemolysis (coughing up blood), and in some cases, chest pain. NSCLC is the most common type of lung cancer compared to Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC) and makes up about 80% to 85% of all lung cancer cases. NSCLC typically has larger cells and grows a lot slower than SCLC, but there are still many concerns. Many modern treatments and advancements have helped with the treatment as well as recovery of patients with NSCLC.

Background and Mechanism

NSCLC occurs when an excess of abnormal cell production forms in the lungs. This abnormal amount of cells can be caused by changes in DNA as well as other factors. There are three common subtypes of NSCLC which include: Adenocarcinoma, Squamous cell carcinoma, & Large cell carcinoma. Adenocarcinoma is the most common subtype of NSCLC and starts in the tissue that lines the outside of the lungs. Squamous cell carcinoma is the next most common type of NSCLC after Adenocarcinoma, and it starts in the lungs in the thin cells that line the breathing tube. Lastly, Large cell carcinoma is the 3rd most common type of NSCLC and it starts in the outer regions of the lungs. A risk factor is something that increases the chance of a disease occuring. Risk factors of NSCLC include smoking, heavy metals and chemicals, radon gas, secondhand smoke, other lung illnesses, and family history.

Diagnosis & Traditional Treatment

NSCLC is diagnosed using a series of tests that include imaging tests, sputum cytology, and occasionally biopsies. Imaging tests are tests that involve taking pictures of the inside of the body. These may include X-rays, MRI’s, CT scans, and PET scans. Sputum cytology involves studying the fluid coughed up from the lungs (sputum) and studying its cells under a microscope to determine if there are traces or signs of lung cancer cells. Biopsies are surgeries or procedures used to gain a small sample of tissue from a part of the body that is used in the lab to test for diseases or traces of cancers. Treatment for NSCLC uses a wide variety of strategies and options depending on the subgroup and severity of the cancer. These treatments can include surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy. Surgery is mainly used if the cancer is only in the lungs and has not spread as much. Surgery involves removing the areas infected by the cancer and some tissue around it to prevent the cancer from occurring again. If the lung cancer is much larger and spread across a larger area, chemotherapy and radiation therapy are more commonly used. Radiation treats the cancer with powerful energy beams that are directed towards precise points in the body to remove cancer cells. Chemotherapy involves using strong medicines to remove cancer cells and are more commonly administered through the vein. Targeted therapy is similar to chemotherapy, but involves using medicine to attack specific chemicals within cancer cells. Lastly, immunotherapy involves using medicine to help the body’s immune system target and attack cancer cells on its own. All of these treatments are traditionally used, but are constantly changing due to new medical advancements.

Recent advancements

New advancements in NSCLC treatment focuses mainly on precision medicine, developing new therapies, using medical drugs to treat cancer, and exploring new strategies to overall help NSCLC research.

Conclusion

NSCLC is the most common type of lung cancer that humans can get, and NSCLC can have various sub groups within it. There are many risk factors that can cause NSCLC to occur or increase the risk of having NSCLC. There are many different treatment and diagnosis techniques used by doctors to help patients with NSCLC. Advancements in medicine and research can help patients with NSCLC and recovery overall.
